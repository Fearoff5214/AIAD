# India GST Compliance Knowledge Pack

## GST Foundations
- GST is a destination-based indirect tax with CGST, SGST/UTGST, and IGST components.
- Intra-state supply applies CGST + SGST/UTGST; inter-state supply applies IGST.
- Taxability depends on nature of supply, place of supply, registration status, and HSN/SAC classification.
- HSN (Harmonised System of Nomenclature) codes classify goods; SAC (Services Accounting Code) classify services.
- GST registration is mandatory when turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (goods) or ₹20 lakh (services); ₹10 lakh for special category states.

## GST Rate Structure and Classification
- Common rate buckets: nil/exempt (0%), 5%, 12%, 18%, and 28%.
- Essential items like unprocessed food grains, milk, eggs are nil rated.
- FMCG, electronics, and services typically attract 18%.
- Luxury goods, tobacco, aerated drinks attract 28% + cess.
- GST rates must be tied to correct HSN/SAC codes and current CBIC notifications.
- Non-GST and exempt items must not be mixed with taxable items in computations.

## E-invoice Mandatory Fields
- E-invoice is mandatory for taxpayers with turnover above the notified threshold (currently ₹5 crore).
- Mandatory fields for e-invoice: Supplier GSTIN, Recipient GSTIN (if registered), Invoice Number, Invoice Date, Invoice Type, Place of Supply (state code), HSN/SAC code for each line item, Taxable Value, GST rate, CGST/SGST/IGST amount, Total Invoice Value.
- The Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) generates the IRN (Invoice Reference Number) and signed QR code.
- IRN is a unique 64-character hash. Once generated, the invoice cannot be modified.
- Validate before submission: party GST category, HSN/SAC codes, item tax template, and place of supply.
- E-invoice cancellation must be done within 24 hours of generation on the IRP.
- B2C transactions (supply to unregistered persons) do not require e-invoice.
- Export invoices require e-invoice if the supplier is notified.

## E-way Bill Core Compliance
- E-way bill is required for movement of goods worth more than ₹50,000 (some states have lower limits).
- E-way bill has two parts: Part A (transaction details) and Part B (transporter/vehicle details).
- Part A: GSTIN of supplier and recipient, place of dispatch, place of delivery, invoice number/date, value of goods, HSN code, reason for transport.
- Part B: Transporter ID or vehicle number.
- E-way bill validity: 1 day per 200 km for regular cargo; 1 day per 20 km for ODC/over-dimensional cargo.
- E-way bill can be generated by supplier, recipient, or transporter.
- Cancellation within 24 hours if goods are not dispatched.
- E-way bill is NOT required for goods exempted under Rule 138(14) of CGST Rules (e.g., fresh fruits, vegetables, milk).

## Credit Note GST Compliance
- A credit note is issued when goods are returned, there is a price reduction, or the original invoice had excess tax charged.
- Credit notes must be issued within the earlier of: (a) 30 September of the year following the financial year of supply, or (b) date of filing the annual return.
- Mandatory fields in a credit note: supplier GSTIN, recipient GSTIN, credit note number, original invoice reference, reason for credit, taxable value adjusted, GST adjusted.
- Credit notes reduce output tax liability of the supplier.
- The recipient must reverse the ITC (Input Tax Credit) if credit note is received.
- Credit note must be reported in GSTR-1 (Table 9B for registered recipients, Table 9B for unregistered).
- Under GST, credit notes are linked to the original e-invoice via the IRN, if applicable.

## Debit Note GST Compliance
- A debit note is issued when additional consideration is charged after the original invoice (e.g., price increase, undercharging).
- Debit notes must be reported in GSTR-1 in the month of issuance.
- Mandatory fields: supplier GSTIN, recipient GSTIN, debit note number, original invoice reference, reason, additional taxable value, additional GST.
- Recipient can claim ITC on debit note in the month it is reported by the supplier in GSTR-1.
- Debit notes increase the supplier's output tax liability.

## Manufacturing Company GST Compliance
- Manufacturing companies must maintain HSN-wise stock records and input-output ratios.
- Job work (section 143 of CGST Act): goods sent for job work must be tracked via delivery challans. Return within 1 year (3 years for capital goods).
- ITC is available on capital goods used in manufacturing taxable supplies.
- Monthly ITC reconciliation between GSTR-2B and purchase register is mandatory.
- Maintain records: purchase invoices, job work challans, stock movement registers, and tax payment challans.
- Reverse charge mechanism (RCM) applies on certain purchases — ensure self-invoicing and RCM payment.
- Annual GSTR-9 filing required; GSTR-9C (reconciliation statement) if turnover exceeds ₹5 crore.
- Inverted duty structure (where input GST > output GST) qualifies for refund under Rule 89.

## Wholesale Company GST Monthly Records
- Monthly GSTR-1 filing: report all outward supplies, B2B invoice-wise, B2C aggregate, credit/debit notes, exports.
- GSTR-3B: summary return with tax liability payment — due by 20th of the following month.
- GSTR-2B reconciliation: match purchase invoices with auto-populated ITC data from suppliers.
- Reconcile any mismatch between GSTR-2B ITC and books — do not claim excess ITC.
- Maintain buyer-wise GSTIN ledger for ITC verification.
- E-way bill register for all consignments above ₹50,000.
- High-volume invoicing: ensure unique sequential invoice numbering within each financial year.
- Distributor pricing rules: if selling at different rates to different buyers, ensure GST is computed on the actual transaction value, not MRP (unless applicable).
- GSTR-9 annual return — consolidates all monthly returns.

## ITC (Input Tax Credit) Rules
- ITC is available on goods and services used in the course of business for making taxable supplies.
- ITC blocked under Section 17(5): motor vehicles (certain), food and beverages, club memberships, health/life insurance (unless statutory), works contract services for immovable property.
- Conditions for ITC: valid tax invoice, goods/services received, tax paid by supplier, GSTR-3B filed.
- ITC must be reversed if payment to supplier not made within 180 days (re-claimed when paid).
- Annual ITC reconciliation via GSTR-9 vs GSTR-2B mismatch report.

## GST Returns Filing
- GSTR-1: Monthly/quarterly outward supply details. Monthly for large taxpayers; quarterly for QRMP scheme.
- GSTR-3B: Monthly summary return with tax payment.
- GSTR-2B: Auto-populated ITC statement (read-only) for purchase reconciliation.
- GSTR-9: Annual return due by 31 December of the following financial year.
- GSTR-9C: Reconciliation statement (self-certified) if turnover > ₹5 crore.
- Late fee: ₹50/day (₹20/day for nil returns) up to maximum of 0.04% of turnover.
- Interest on late tax payment: 18% per annum on outstanding tax liability.

## Invoice Mandatory Fields (GST Invoice Rules)
- Mandatory fields for a valid GST tax invoice: supplier name, address, GSTIN; recipient name, address, GSTIN (for B2B); consecutive serial number; date of issue; HSN/SAC code; description of goods/services; quantity and unit; taxable value; applicable discounts; rate and amount of CGST, SGST, IGST; place of supply; whether tax is payable on reverse charge; signature or digital signature of supplier.
- For B2C transactions above ₹2.5 lakh: recipient name, address, and destination state are mandatory.
- Bill of Supply: issued for exempt supplies or by composition taxpayers (no GST charged).
- Self-invoice: issued by recipient for RCM purchases from unregistered suppliers.

## HSN Code Requirements
- HSN codes are mandatory on invoices for GST-registered suppliers.
- Turnover up to ₹5 crore: 4-digit HSN required.
- Turnover above ₹5 crore: 6-digit HSN required.
- Services use SAC codes (6 digits).
- Incorrect HSN can lead to mismatch in GSTR-1 vs GSTR-2B reconciliation.

## Place of Supply Rules
- For goods: place of supply = place where goods are delivered.
- For services: generally the location of the recipient.
- Special rules for specific services: telecommunications, banking, insurance, transportation, accommodation, etc.
- Incorrect place of supply = wrong tax type (IGST vs CGST+SGST) = compliance risk.

## Composition Scheme
- Available for small taxpayers with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh for special states) for goods; ₹50 lakh for services.
- Composition taxpayers pay tax at fixed rates: 1% for manufacturers, 5% for restaurants, 6% for service providers.
- Cannot collect GST from customers — must pay from own pocket.
- Cannot claim ITC.
- Must file CMP-08 quarterly and GSTR-4 annually.

## GST Refunds
- Refund of excess ITC for exporters (with/without payment of tax).
- Refund for inverted duty structure (output GST < input GST).
- Refund of tax paid on deemed exports.
- File RFD-01 on GST portal within 2 years from relevant date.
- Provisional refund of 90% for zero-rated supplies within 7 days.

## Export and Zero-Rated Supply
- Exports of goods and services are zero-rated (GST = 0%) under Section 16 of IGST Act.
- Two options for exporters: (1) Export under LUT (Letter of Undertaking) without payment of IGST and claim refund of ITC; (2) Pay IGST and claim refund of IGST paid.
- LUT is filed on GST portal (Form RFD-11) annually.
- SEZ supplies are also zero-rated.

## Common GST Errors to Avoid
- Charging IGST on intra-state transactions (should be CGST + SGST).
- Applying GST on nil-rated or exempt items (e.g., unbranded rice, wheat, milk, eggs, fresh vegetables).
- Incorrect HSN code leading to wrong tax rate.
- Not issuing credit note for returned goods or price revisions.
- Claiming ITC on blocked items (Section 17(5)).
- Missing e-invoice generation when mandatory.
- Not reconciling GSTR-2B with purchase register before filing GSTR-3B.
- Filing returns late — attracts interest and late fees.

## GSTR-1 Filing Details
- GSTR-1 must be filed by the 11th of the following month for monthly filers.
- QRMP scheme filers: IFF (Invoice Furnishing Facility) by 13th for months 1 and 2; GSTR-1 quarterly by 13th after quarter end.
- B2B invoices (supply to registered persons): report invoice-wise in Table 4A/4B.
- B2C large invoices (above ₹2.5 lakh, inter-state): report invoice-wise in Table 5.
- B2C small (below ₹2.5 lakh or intra-state): report aggregate state-wise in Table 7.
- Exports: Table 6A (with payment of tax), Table 6B (without payment of tax).
- Nil-rated, exempt, non-GST supplies: Table 8.
- Amendments in subsequent month: Table 9A (invoices), 9B (credit/debit notes), 9C (B2C amendments).
- HSN summary: mandatory in Table 12 with quantity and taxable value for each HSN code.

## RCM (Reverse Charge Mechanism) Rules
- RCM applies when the purchaser is liable to pay GST instead of the supplier.
- Mandatory RCM categories: legal services by advocates, GTA (Goods Transport Agency), security services, import of services, purchase from unregistered dealers for certain categories.
- Self-invoice must be issued by recipient for RCM purchases.
- ITC on RCM: recipient can claim ITC after paying RCM tax in GSTR-3B.
- RCM liability reported in GSTR-3B Table 3.1(d).
- Cash ledger must have sufficient balance for RCM payment; cannot be offset from ITC credit for RCM payment.

## E-Invoice Validation Checklist
- Supplier GSTIN: 15-character alphanumeric (format: 2-digit state + 10-char PAN + 1-char entity + Z + 1-char check).
- Valid invoice serial number: unique within financial year, max 16 characters, alphanumeric with /-, no special characters.
- Place of supply: 2-digit state code matching buyer's state.
- HSN code: 4-digit (turnover <₹5cr), 6-digit (turnover >₹5cr).
- IRN generation is mandatory before dispatching goods for applicable taxpayers.
- QR code on invoice: must contain IRN, supplier GSTIN, invoice number, date, value.
- Cancellation only possible within 24 hours; after 24 hours issue credit note.
- Amendment to e-invoice is NOT allowed; cancel and create new invoice.

## GST on Services (SAC Codes)
- SAC 9954: Construction services — 12% (affordable housing) or 18%.
- SAC 9961/9962: Wholesale/retail trade — 18%.
- SAC 9963: Accommodation, food and beverage — 5% (restaurant), 12%/18% (hotel based on tariff).
- SAC 9971: Financial services — 18%.
- SAC 9972: Real estate — 5% (under-construction residential), 12% (commercial).
- SAC 9981-9997: Professional services — 18%.
- SAC 9985: IT services/software — 18%.
- SAC 9993: Education services — mostly exempt; some 18%.
- SAC 9991: Health care — mostly exempt.
- Export of services: zero-rated (0% GST), file LUT on portal.

## Input Service Distributor (ISD)
- ISD distributes ITC of common input services to branches.
- ISD registration required separately; file GSTR-6 monthly.
- Distribution based on turnover ratio of branches.
- ITC received via ISD must be reflected in GSTR-2B of recipient branches.

## Annual Return GSTR-9 Key Points
- Due date: 31 December of next financial year.
- Mandatory for regular taxpayers with turnover >₹2 crore (below ₹2cr optional but recommended).
- GSTR-9C (reconciliation) required if turnover >₹5 crore (self-certified from FY 2020-21 onward).
- Amendments not possible after filing GSTR-9.
- Common errors: ITC reversal mismatch, RCM not declared, HSN summary errors.
